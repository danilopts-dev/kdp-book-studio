# How This Book Works

Welcome! This book has one part for each of the eight nights of Hanukkah. Read one night at a time, right before you light the candles, and you're done for the day. Most nights take about 20 to 30 minutes for the story and a few puzzles. You never have to do every page, so pick the ones that look fun and come back to the rest any time.

You don't have to start on Night 1. If tonight is the third night of Hanukkah, turn to Night 3 and start there. Every night stands on its own, so nobody is behind.

Each night has two levels of activities. Look for the stars next to the title:

**★** activities are for younger puzzle solvers, around ages 6 to 7.

**★★** activities are for kids who want more of a challenge, around ages 8 to 10.

Nobody has to pick just one level. A seven-year-old who loves a challenge can try the ★★ page, and an older kid having an off night can go back to ★. The stars are a starting point, not a rule.

Most of the story and the "Before the Candles" page are for reading together as a family. The puzzle pages are built for a kid to do mostly on their own, with a grown-up nearby to help if a word is tricky or a maze gets frustrating.

# Lighting the Hanukkiah

**Pick a spot.** The hanukkiah goes where people outside can see it, in a window facing the street or near the front door. If that doesn't work for your home, a sturdy table inside is fine. Keep it away from curtains, and make sure a grown-up is close by whenever the candles are burning.

**Set up the candles.** On Night 1, put one candle in the holder on the far right. Each night after that, add one more candle to the left of the last one. The shamash gets its own holder, usually higher or off to the side, and it doesn't count as one of the eight.

![](illustrations/FM1.png "3.4in")

**Light them.** Say the blessings first (you'll find them on Night 1). Then use the lit shamash to light tonight's candles, starting with the newest one on the left and moving right. When they're all lit, put the shamash back in its holder and let the candles burn down on their own.

**Friday night:** light the Hanukkah candles first, then the Shabbat candles.

Families have their own customs, like exactly when to light the shamash or where the hanukkiah stands. If your family does something differently, do it your way.
